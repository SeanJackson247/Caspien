Caspien before and after @inline

Optimised Caspien programs, quick benchmark, 47 programs/variants. Measured 4 Oct 2026, one program at a time, before then after, back to back.

What changed

  • Compiler: @inline and @dont(inline) on a function. @inline inlines every call that is safe to inline, even with function-inlining: off and past the preset's size and growth limits; the safety rules (no @async, no recursion cycle, scalar parameters, ...) still apply. The optimizer reports what it did ([note] file:line - @inline: f inlined at N call sites) and warns about calls it could not inline.
  • Benchmark programs: @inline on the six helper functions of nbody_natural_f64 (length, speedSquared, kinetic, potential, attract, drift) and on advance in nbody_arr and nbody_arr_f64. Nothing else: in every other optimised program the aggressive preset already inlines every call that matters (checked by listing the calls left in the optimised bytecode), and trying @inline where calls did remain (merkletrees) or @unroll (hand-unrolled by 4: 3%) gave nothing measurable.
  • Stdlib: @inline on DynamicArray.get / set / setPtr and String.charAt / setCharAt.

How it was measured

Quick benchmark, Caspien only, optimised builds (every optimisation on, loop-unrolling: aggressive, function-inlining: aggressive), best of 3 runs. For each program the old tree (commit 4fde78a: old programs, old stdlib, old compiler) was measured first and the new tree straight after, so slow drift of the machine hits both. Outputs of every row match the C reference.

Run time per Caspien program, after versus before

Each bar is (after / before − 1). The shaded band is ±5%, about the run-to-run noise. A bar is coloured only when it is outside the band and outside 1.5× that row's own run-to-run spread; grey bars are within noise. faster slower no measurable change   ✎ = the program source was edited (@inline); every other row is the same source and the same assembly, so its movement is noise.

5 of 47 rows moved outside the noise band.

The stdlib @inline, under the other inlining presets

The benchmark config uses function-inlining: aggressive, which already inlines everything the stdlib marks, so the benchmark rows above cannot show the stdlib change. Compiled with off or conservative (the shipped config is off) the naive programs, which call DynamicArray.get/set and String.charAt/setCharAt in their inner loops, get much faster.

Caveats

  • Machine noise is 10–30% between measurements minutes apart (an earlier before/after taken 25 minutes apart showed sieve 39% slower with byte-identical assembly). Back-to-back pairing reduces it but does not remove it; rows inside the grey band are unchanged.
  • Only the three marked n-body programs were edited. For the unedited ones I compared the generated assembly of the old and new compiler on sieve (safe, naive) and graph (safe): identical, so their movement is machine noise; I did not compare the rest.
  • The stdlib chart uses best of 3 per preset, measured one after the other, not the full harness.
  • @unroll was not used in any program: nearly every hot loop has a variable bound, which the unroller cannot unroll, and unrolling a loop with literal bounds was worth about 2–3% (loop-unrolling aggressive vs off).
  • Linux/x86-64 only; Windows and Wine were not measured.
